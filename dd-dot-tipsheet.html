The Pre-DOT Card Save
The fast levers that move your numbers before your physical — from a former driver, built for the road.
The 4 numbers they check — and how to move each
1 · Blood Pressure
The one that fails the most drivers

This moves faster than anything else on the exam. In the 2–4 weeks before your physical:

  • Cut the energy drinks and soda — swap to water and black coffee
  • Walk 10 minutes at every fuel stop; it drops resting BP over weeks
  • Ease way back on salty fried truck-stop food the week of the exam
  • Sleep on a real schedule the 3 nights before — poor sleep spikes BP
2 · Weight & Waist
Where the long haul catches up with you

You won't overhaul this in a week, but you can trend it the right way and show effort:

  • Protein first at every stop — grilled/rotisserie chicken over the fried case
  • Cut the liquid calories; that alone drops a few pounds a month for most drivers
  • The daily "Undo the Drive" reset + 3 short sessions a week (in your free program)
3 · Blood Sugar
The one that turns a 2-year card into a 1-year

Sugar and refined carbs on the road are the usual driver here:

  • Drop the sugary drinks first — biggest single lever
  • Pair carbs with protein and walk after you eat to blunt the spike
  • If you're already flagged, talk to your doc early — don't let it surprise you at the exam
4 · Vision & Hearing
Easy to pass — if you prep, easy to fail if you don't

Not fitness, but drivers lose cards over these for dumb reasons:

  • Bring your glasses/contacts and wear them for the exam
  • If you've noticed changes, get checked before the physical, not at it
The week of your exam
✓ Card-Save Checklist
  • Water only for 48 hours — no energy drinks
  • Walk daily, even 10 minutes
  • Sleep 7+ hours the 3 nights before
  • Skip the salt-bomb meals 2 days out
  • Bring glasses/contacts + your med list
  • Show up rested, not rushed — stress raises BP right there in the chair
Straight talk: this is general prep info from a certified trainer and former driver — not medical advice, and it won't fake a number you haven't earned. If you've got a diagnosed condition or your numbers are already high, work with your doctor early. The point is to walk into that exam having done the work, not scrambling the night before.
